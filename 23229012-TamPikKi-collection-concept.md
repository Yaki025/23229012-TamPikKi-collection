**Title: The Digital Collection of Loss: 00s Kid Childhood Memories**

This collection is a digital archive dedicated to a fading era of innocence, capturing the collective childhood memories of the 00s generation. As we, the 00s kids, gradually step into the realities of adulthood, the pure, simple, and tangible joys of our youth are slowly slipping away. Driven by rapid technological advancement, the physical toys we played with, the nostalgic snacks we tasted at tuck shops, and the analog media we consumed have been largely replaced by sterile digital screens and advanced electronics. That effortless, physical happiness has vanished from our daily lives, marking the quiet departure of an entire generational epoch.

To capture this phenomenon, the first nineteen records share a unified recorder name, which is "00s kid." This choice reflects a deliberate argument. This collection does not merely document an individual nostalgia. Instead, it serves as a shared cultural repository for our entire generation. Across these nineteen encounters with different everyday artifacts, ranging from discontinued snacks and virtual pets to stationery store treasures, what binds them together is the collective loss of materiality. They highlight how physical interactions have been traded for digital efficiency, leaving behind a void that current technology cannot replicate. While curating and assembling this collection, I found myself deeply touched while reflecting back on my own childhood.

However, the twentieth record, which is the final and most distinct piece in the collection, deliberately breaks this collective pattern. Instead of another shared artifact, it is a text record containing a personal birthday letter I wrote to my future self during my first year of secondary school. At thirteen, my worldview was shaped by innocent dreams, creative aspirations, and a naive curiosity about the future. By contrasting the heartfelt words of that thirteen-year-old self with who I have become today, this final card shifts the scale from a broad generational narrative to an intimate and vulnerable self-reflection.

While the first nineteen cards capture what our generation lost collectively, this final record preserves what I lost individually. It stands as a unique anchor within the system as a dedicated trace of my own childhood, marking the exact moment a child began transforming into an adult.

Ultimately, what this collection cannot hold is the physical and emotional weight of the past itself. A digital image, a sound file, or a scanned letter can store data, but they cannot resurrect the texture of the materials or the heartbeat of the exact moment they were lived. Through these twenty records, the archive admits its own limitations. It preserves the evidence of our vanishing youth, but the living feeling of being a 00s kid is gone forever.
